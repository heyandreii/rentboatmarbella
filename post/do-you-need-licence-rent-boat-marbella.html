Do you need a licence to rent a boat in Marbella?

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

If you are planning a day on the water, this is usually the first question that comes up: do you need a licence to rent a boat in Marbella? The short answer is no. With us, every boat hire with licence on the Costa del Sol works the same way: the qualified person is already aboard, because a professional skipper is included in the rate. You step aboard, choose the plan for the day and enjoy it; handling the boat, the route and the safety side is their job. Below we explain why that is, and what Spanish law would ask of you if you ever wanted to take the helm yourself.

The short answer: with a skipper on board, no qualification is needed

A nautical licence is a requirement for whoever is in command of the vessel, not for the people sailing as guests. On a skippered charter, the qualified person is part of the service: a professional holding the licence and the certification the law requires to carry passengers, who knows the boat and is responsible for it throughout the trip.

So when you book with us, neither you nor anyone in your group needs to produce anything beyond an identity document. No exam, no course, no paperwork. It is the same logic by which you do not need a driving licence to take a taxi.

What Spanish law requires if you want to helm the boat yourself

Bareboat hire — where you rent the vessel and skipper it yourself — is a different matter entirely, and that is where qualifications come in. In Spain recreational nautical licences are governed by Royal Decree 875/2014, and each one authorises a specific size of boat and a specific distance from the coast.

Licencia de Navegación (the entry-level permit)

The most basic of them. It is obtained through a short theory-and-practice course with no official exam, and covers vessels up to 6 metres in length, in daylight only and within 2 nautical miles of a port or shelter. Enough for a small runabout; nowhere near enough for a yacht.

Patrón para Navegación Básica (PNB)

This one requires an exam. It covers vessels up to 8 metres and allows you to go 5 nautical miles offshore. It is an uncommon middle rung — most people who study for a licence go straight to the next one.

Patrón de Embarcaciones de Recreo (PER)

The standard licence in Spain. It covers vessels up to 15 metres and navigation up to 12 nautical miles from the coast. It involves a course, a written exam, compulsory safety and navigation practicals and a medical check. Between the syllabus, the fees and the practical hours, earning it takes weeks and is not cheap.

Patrón de Yate and Capitán de Yate

For larger vessels and offshore passages there are the Patrón de Yate and Capitán de Yate certificates, with progressively higher requirements. These are qualifications for experienced navigators.

One important nuance sits on top of all this: carrying paying passengers is a commercial activity, and it requires a professional qualification rather than a recreational one. Even holding a PER, you could not charter your boat out and skipper it for guests.

Why a skippered charter solves it

Framed that way, the maths is simple. If you want a day at sea in Marbella and hold no licence — or hold one but have no wish to spend the day watching the helm, the anchor and the August traffic in and out of Puerto Banús — a skippered charter takes care of all of it.

Our 15-metre De Antonio D50 leaves from Puerto Banús with a professional skipper always on board. They plan the route around the day's wind, pick the anchorages, handle the manoeuvring in the marina and keep an eye on the group's safety. You decide the plan: coves, swimming, lunch on board or a sunset.

The skipper is included in the price, not added at the end. Rates are €1,200 for 2 hours, €1,800 for a half day (4 hours) and €3,000 for the full day (8 hours), always for the whole boat for groups of up to 10 people, with skipper, fuel and VAT included. No licence needed does not mean no one is in charge

The fact that you need no qualification does not mean the day runs itself. The skipper is what makes everything else work:

  • They set the day's route according to the wind and sea state, and adjust it as conditions change.
  • They choose the anchorages, check the depth and the nature of the seabed and make sure the boat sits safely while you swim.
  • They manage safety: onboard equipment, lifejackets, the bathing platform and the basic ground rules when children are aboard.
  • They know this coast, so they know where dolphins tend to appear, which coves are quiet at which hour and where the sunset looks best.

In practice, that is the difference between renting a boat and having a genuinely good day at sea.

"Without a licence" versus "without a skipper"

Two phrases get used almost interchangeably in search results, and they mean opposite things. Without a licence means you need no qualification because a professional is aboard. Without a skipper means the reverse: you are handed the keys and navigate yourself, with whatever licence applies and all the responsibility on you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a licence to rent a boat in Marbella?

No. On our charter the professional skipper is included in the rate, so no guest needs any nautical qualification. All you need to bring on board is your ID card or passport.

What licence would I need to helm the boat myself?

Spanish recreational licences are set out in Royal Decree 875/2014. The Licencia de Navegación covers boats up to 6 metres, within 2 nautical miles of shore and in daylight only; the PNB extends to 8 metres and 5 miles; and the PER, by far the most common, allows up to 15 metres and 12 miles. Larger vessels require the Patrón de Yate or Capitán de Yate.

Can someone in my group take the helm during the trip?

The skipper is responsible for the vessel at all times and decides what is possible. If conditions are good and they judge it safe, it is common for guests to take the helm for a while in open water, always under supervision.
