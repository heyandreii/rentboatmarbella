Boat rental on the Costa del Sol: why to set off from Puerto Banús

Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

Boat rental on the Costa del Sol comes with a detail that rarely gets explained properly: where you set off from changes everything. This is more than a hundred kilometres of coastline, from Manilva to Nerja, with marinas spread along the whole of it, and your departure point decides how much of it you actually get to see. This guide walks the coast stretch by stretch from the water, and explains why — for a trip of 2, 4 or 8 hours — Puerto Banús is the best base for a premium boat charter.

The coast, stretch by stretch, seen from the water

From the sea the Costa del Sol reads differently. What looks from land like one continuous run of development separates, from the water, into stretches with distinct character.

Estepona and Manilva (the western end)

The least built-up part of the western coast. Wide beaches, few boats and some genuinely clear water out towards Punta de la Chullera. It is beautiful, but it is far: getting there and back eats most of a short trip.

San Pedro, Guadalmina and El Saladillo

Just west of Puerto Banús, minutes away. Broad open beaches, easy anchoring over sand and very few people. This is the natural shelter on easterly-wind days, when the eastern side of the bay turns choppy.

Marbella and the Golden Mile

The most recognisable stretch of the whole coast from the sea: villas, beach clubs, the Puente Romano and La Concha rising straight up behind. Clean sand underneath and gentle depths. It has the best light at sunset, and it is where most of the photographs get taken.

Río Real, Las Chapas and Cabopino

East of Marbella the coast opens up and empties out. Las Chapas has that characteristic green-blue water and, further on, the Artola dunes and the Cabopino watchtower make up the prettiest protected landscape on the coast. Transparent water over golden sand.

Calahonda, La Cala de Mijas and Fuengirola

Now within Mijas, coves mix with rocky stretches that bring the seabed to life and make snorkelling genuinely worthwhile. Fuengirola sits at the far end and, as with Estepona, distance decides: it is full-day territory.

Why Puerto Banús is the best base

Lay the coast out on a map and the advantage of Puerto Banús is purely geometric: it sits in the middle. From its entrance there is interesting coastline in both directions, and that changes how much you get out of a trip.

Leave from a marina at either end — Estepona or Fuengirola — and half your time goes on reaching the good stretch and coming back. From Banús the Golden Mile is minutes away, the first coves too, and the skipper can decide on the day whether to head east or west depending on the wind. That freedom to choose is exactly what separates a great day at sea from an average one.

There is a second, more practical reason: access. Puerto Banús is 10 minutes from central Marbella, around 45 from Málaga airport, with parking right by the pontoon. For a group flying in, that solves the logistics of the day.

And a third: the marina itself. Heading out through Banús, with the yachts moored on both sides and the promenade busy, is part of the experience. No other port on this coast has it.

When to go: wind, season and time of day

Two winds govern this coast. With the levante (easterly) the sea gets choppy on the eastern side of the bay and it pays to head towards Guadalmina and San Pedro; with the poniente (westerly) it is the other way round and the Cabopino side is better sheltered. The skipper decides each morning from the forecast, which is precisely why setting off from a central point is a real advantage rather than a brochure line.

As for season, July and August bring the flattest sea and the fullest coves; from October to May there is far more availability and the coast is close to empty. And by time of day: mornings give the smoothest water, afternoons the best light.

Frequently asked questions

Which ports can you rent a boat from on the Costa del Sol?

The coast has several marinas: Estepona, Puerto Banús, Marbella, Cabopino, La Cala de Mijas and Fuengirola. Puerto Banús is the most central of them and leaves the most navigable coastline on either side, which is why it works best as a base for a 2, 4 or 8-hour trip.

Can you reach Estepona or Fuengirola in a single trip?

Yes, but the duration matters. Four hours comfortably covers the near ends of the bay; to reach Estepona or Fuengirola with time left to anchor and swim, the full 8-hour day is the realistic choice.

Do you need a licence to rent a boat on the Costa del Sol?

Not with us. A professional skipper is included on every trip, so no guest needs a nautical qualification. All you need is an identity document.
