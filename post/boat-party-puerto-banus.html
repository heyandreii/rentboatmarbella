Boat party in Puerto Banús: how it works on a private yacht

Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

Search for a Puerto Banús boat party and you will find two completely different things sold under the same words. One is a ticketed party boat: a big vessel, a hundred strangers, a fixed two-hour loop and whatever music the operator is playing. The other is a private yacht charter, where the boat is yours alone for the day. This guide is about the second kind — how a private boat party in Marbella actually works, what you control, what it costs and why most groups who have tried both do not go back.

Private yacht vs. ticketed party boat

The distinction matters more than the price tag suggests. On a ticketed party boat you buy a seat. You share the deck with a crowd, the playlist belongs to someone else, the route is fixed, the departure time is fixed, and the bar is a queue. It is cheap per head, and for a certain kind of night out that is exactly the point.

A private charter inverts every one of those variables. The yacht carries your group only — up to 10 people — and the day is built around what you want. You pick the departure time, the route, how long you stay anchored, when you swim and what plays through the speakers. Nobody queues for anything, because there is no one else aboard.

The economics look different too, but not as different as the headline numbers imply. A private charter is €1,200 for 2 hours, €1,800 for a half day of 4 hours and €3,000 for the full 8-hour day — for the whole boat, with skipper, fuel and VAT included. Split across a group of eight or ten, a two-hour sunset party lands close to what a premium ticketed trip charges per person, except you get the entire yacht.

The music: your playlist, your volume

This is the detail that decides the atmosphere, and it is the one you lose completely on a ticketed boat. The onboard sound system connects over Bluetooth, so whoever in your group has the best taste takes charge of the phone and that is the end of the discussion.

Volume works the same way. Loud while you are running along the coast, down to background level once you are anchored and people want to talk or swim. On a shared boat that dial belongs to the operator; here it belongs to you.

Food and drink on board

You can bring your own food and drink with no corkage charge — for most groups that means champagne, beers and something picked up in Puerto Banús on the way to the pontoon. Water and ice are already on board.

If you would rather not deal with any of it, extended catering is available as a €180 extra and arrives set up before you board. For a bachelor or hen party arriving straight from a flight, that is usually money well spent.

The jet ski and what else is on board

A jet ski can be added for €250, and it is the extra that most changes the shape of the afternoon. Anchored off a cove, it turns a swim stop into an hour of everyone taking turns, and it is the single best source of footage from the day.

Included at no extra cost, and often forgotten in the planning: a paddle board, snorkelling gear, the bathing platform at the stern and full safety equipment. The platform is what makes the swim stops work — getting in and out of the water is effortless, which sounds trivial until you have tried it over a ladder.

The route: where a Puerto Banús party actually goes

Leaving the marina, the coast opens in both directions and the skipper picks the plan around the day's wind. Heading east you have the Golden Mile, the Marbella bay and, further along, the clear water off Artola and Cabopino. Heading west, the beaches past Guadalmina are wider and emptier — the better call on an easterly wind day.

A typical two-hour party run is a stretch along the Golden Mile with the music up, one anchored stop for swimming and the jet ski, and the return into Puerto Banús at sunset. Four hours adds a second cove, a proper meal on deck and a lot less clock-watching. The skipper knows which anchorages stay quiet at which hour, which in August is the whole game.

Bachelor and hen parties

These are the two occasions the private format suits best, largely because both live or die on not being interrupted. A yacht with only your group aboard is a very different day from a shared deck.

Practical details worth knowing

  • Departure is from Puerto Banús; you are on the pontoon and away within minutes.
  • No licence is required — a professional skipper is included on every trip.
  • Capacity is 10 people, and the rate is the same whether you are four or ten.
  • Book early in summer. Sunset slots from June to September go weeks ahead, and Saturdays go first.
  • Bring swimwear, a towel, sun cream and a card — everything else is aboard.

The boat itself is a 15-metre De Antonio D50, which is worth a look if you want to see the deck layout before you decide how many people to invite.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a private boat party in Puerto Banús cost?

Rates are for the whole boat, not per person: €1,200 for 2 hours, €1,800 for a half day of 4 hours and €3,000 for the full 8-hour day, for groups of up to 10 people. Skipper, fuel and VAT are included.

Can we bring our own music and drinks?

Yes. The onboard sound system connects by Bluetooth, so the playlist is yours, and you can bring your own food and drink with no corkage charge. Extended catering is available as a €180 extra.

What is the difference from a party boat ticket?

A party boat sells individual tickets, so you share the deck, the music and the schedule with people you have never met. A private charter is the whole yacht for your group alone: you choose the route, the timings and the playlist, and no one else comes aboard.
