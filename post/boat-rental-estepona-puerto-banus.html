Boat rental in Estepona: the private charter is twenty minutes away

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

If you are looking for boat rental in Estepona, the first thing worth knowing is that the boat does not have to leave from Estepona for the day to work. Our home port is Puerto Banús, 22 kilometres away — about a twenty minute drive. And in practice those twenty minutes buy you considerably more coastline than leaving from Estepona itself would.

Estepona to Puerto Banús: the real drive

It is about 22 km port to port. On the AP-7 toll motorway that is 20 to 25 minutes at any time of year. On the coastal A-7 it takes a little longer, and in July and August quite a lot longer: that is the road everybody uses to get to the beach.

Parking is inside the marina, right by the pontoon, so you do not need to budget extra time for the car. In high season, do leave a margin — Puerto Banús on an August Saturday fills up.

For perspective: it is a shorter drive than the one plenty of people in Marbella make to get to the Cabopino coves. It is a local hop, not a journey.

How does departure work?

Home port: Puerto Banús. Departure from Estepona available on request. The boat is berthed in Puerto Banús and that is where the charter starts by default. If you would rather board in Estepona, write to us with your date and we will look into it: it is something we assess case by case, not something we can take for granted in advance.

In practice almost everyone drives to Puerto Banús: twenty minutes, parking inside the marina and the boat waiting on the pontoon. No supplements, no transfer times to coordinate and nothing eaten out of your charter by logistics.

The western coves are on your way home

This is the real advantage of coming from Estepona: the good water sits between Estepona and Puerto Banús, not on the far side. When you head west out of Banús, five or ten minutes of cruising puts you in the stretch that is, for you, on the way home.

El Saladillo and Guadalmina

Wide beaches, clean sand bottom and very few boats around. It is the most comfortable anchorage on the whole western stretch, and where you go when the easterly is blowing and the Marbella side is choppy.

San Pedro Alcántara

Right next to Banús, only minutes away. Calm water, a good spot for the first swim and for getting the paddle board out without going far.

Towards the Estepona coast

The stretch between Estepona and Manilva is the most open on the western coast: less built up, with very clean water towards Punta de la Chullera. Getting there with time to anchor, eat and swim needs the full 8-hour day; on a 2 or 4 hour charter it is out of reach.

If you want the detail on each anchorage — seabed, best time of day, when it gets busy — it is all in our guide to the best coves to anchor in Marbella.

The boat: one De Antonio D50 for ten people

There is no fleet to choose from: there is one boat, a 15-metre De Antonio D50 built in 2026 with 2× Mercury V12 600 hp. Which means that when you book, you already know exactly which boat you are getting, who is skippering it and what condition it is in — precisely what you do not know when you book through a listings platform.

It takes up to 10 people comfortably: swim platform aft, bow sunbed and an interior cabin. A professional skipper is included in every charter, so no passenger needs a boat licence.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charter a private boat in Estepona?

Our home port is Puerto Banús, around 22 km from Estepona: a 20 to 25 minute drive on the AP-7. Departure from Estepona is available on request, so if boarding there suits you better, send us your date and we will look into it.

How long does it take to drive from Estepona to Puerto Banús?

About 20 to 25 minutes on the AP-7 toll motorway, and a bit longer on the coastal A-7, which gets busy in July and August. It is roughly 22 km port to port.

Is there parking at Puerto Banús?

Yes, there is parking inside the marina itself, right by the pontoons. In high season leave a margin: the port fills up, especially on August weekends.

Which coves can we reach heading towards Estepona?

Heading west from Puerto Banús you have El Saladillo, Guadalmina and San Pedro, all within a few minutes' cruising. That is the side that holds up best on easterly wind days. To go further, towards the Estepona coast with time to anchor and swim, the realistic option is the full 8-hour day.
